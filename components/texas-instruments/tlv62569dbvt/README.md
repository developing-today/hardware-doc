# Texas Instruments TLV62569DBVT — the main 3.3 V rail

- **Category:** synchronous step-down (buck) converter, 2 A
- **Reference designator on this board:** `U19`
- **Marking in the schematic BOM field:** `TLV62569DBVT` — SOT23-5 (`DBV`), tape-and-reel
- **Owning MCU:** **none.** This rail is unconditionally enabled in hardware; no processor can switch it
- **Research status:** part, package, feedback network and output voltage fully established from the schematic and the TI datasheet. Load figures downstream are estimated and labelled
- **Retrieved:** 2026-08-21; schematic re-read pin-by-pin and power budget worked 2026-08-23

## 1. Identity and what it supplies

`U19` sits in the block literally titled **"DCDC FOR 3V3"** on schematic sheet 1 **[SCH `1_LCD&POWER.png`]**. It converts the board's **5 V** rail down to **3.3 V**, and that 3.3 V rail — net name `3V3` — is the main digital rail for essentially the whole board.

Decoding the ordering suffix **[DOC]**: `TLV62569` base part, `DBV` = SOT23-5, `T` = small tape-and-reel (250 pieces).

Note the **absence of a `P`**. TI ships two variants:

| | **TLV62569** (fitted) | TLV62569**P** |
|---|---|---|
| Packages | **SOT23-5 (DBV)**, SOT563-6 (DRL) | SOT23-6 (DDC), SOT563-6 (DRL) |
| Power-good output | **No** | Yes, open-drain `PG` |
| High-side current limit | **3 A** (DBV) | 3 A (DDC) / 2.5 A (DRL) |

Since the fitted part is the 5-pin DBV, **there is no power-good signal on this board.** Nothing tells the processors that the 3.3 V rail has come up or has sagged; the S3's own brownout detector is the only supervision present.

### 1.1 What is on this rail **[SCH, all five sheets]**

| Consumer | Notes |
|---|---|
| **ESP32-S3R8** (`U2`) | `VDD3P3` via `L2` 2 nH, `VDDA`, `VDD3P3_RTC`, `VDD3P3_CPU`, `VDD_SPI` — the whole chip and its radio |
| **ESP32-U4WDH** (`U14`) | `VDDA`, `VDD3P3` via `L6` 2 nH, `VDD3P3_RTC`, `VDD3P3_CPU`, `VDD_SDIO` — the whole second chip and its radio |

**What is *not* on this rail** — and this is the important half:

- The **PCM5100A DAC** and the **CH445P audio mux** run from `3V3_DAC`, a separate low-noise LDO rail generated by [sgmicro/sgm2036-3.3](../../sgmicro/sgm2036-3.3/README.md) **[SCH sheet 5]**
- Anything on the 5 V rail itself, including the `BATT_ADC` divider and `CN1` pin 1

## 2. Exact wiring **[SCH sheet 1]**

| Pin | Name | Connected to | Detail |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 1 | `EN` | **5 V** | **Hard-tied to the input rail. Always on.** No processor, button or supervisor can disable it |
| 2 | `GND` | GND | |
| 3 | `SW` | `LX1` → `L4` **2.2 µH** → `3V3` | The switch node |
| 4 | `VIN` | **5 V** | `C15` **10 µF** input capacitor |
| 5 | `FB` | `FB1` | Divider midpoint. `C17` **10 µF** output capacitor sits on the `3V3` node |

Feedback divider:

| Component | Value | Role |
|---|---|---|
| `R7` | **100 kΩ** | Upper (`Rh`), `3V3` → `FB1` |
| `R8` | **22.1 kΩ** | Lower (`Rl`), `FB1` → GND |

The schematic even prints the design equation on the sheet: `Vout = 0.6 × (1 + Rh/Rl) = 3.3V` **[SCH]**. Checking it:

```
Vout = 0.6 × (1 + 100 000 / 22 100)
     = 0.6 × (1 + 4.5249)
     = 0.6 × 5.5249
     = 3.315 V
```

So the nominal output is **3.315 V**, 0.45% above 3.30 V. The 0.6 V reference is specified 0.588–0.612 V **[DOC]**, i.e. ±2%, giving a worst-case output window of roughly **3.25 V to 3.38 V** before resistor tolerance. `R8` being a 1%-series value (22.1 kΩ, not 22 kΩ) shows the divider was chosen deliberately rather than approximated.

### 2.1 `EN` tied high — consequences

This is a design decision with real consequences, so it is worth being explicit:

- **There is no software power-down of the 3.3 V rail.** Every low-power strategy on this board has to be a *chip*-level strategy (ESP32 light/deep sleep, DRV2605L `STANDBY`, backlight PWM to zero), never a rail-level one.
- **The buck's 35 µA quiescent current is always being drawn** whenever 5 V is present **[DOC]**. Its 0.1 µA shutdown state is unreachable.
- **Sequencing is fixed:** 3.3 V follows 5 V after the soft-start time (`tSS` = 800 µs typ for DBV **[DOC]**), with no way to delay or stage it.
- On the plus side, `EN` is *terminated*, which the datasheet insists on — *"The EN input must be terminated and should not be left floating"* **[DOC §7.4.1]**.

### 3.1 Two behaviours worth knowing about

**Power Save Mode.** At light load the converter goes discontinuous, drops its switching frequency and minimises current consumption. TI notes the side effect: *"In Power Save Mode, the output voltage rises slightly above the nominal output voltage. This effect is minimized by increasing the output capacitor."* **[DOC §7.3.1]** With only 10 µF of output capacitance, expect a small positive offset and a variable, sub-1.5 MHz switching frequency when the board is idle with the backlight off. **[INF]** That variable frequency is more likely to land in an audible or aliasing-relevant range than the 1.5 MHz PWM frequency is — relevant if you are chasing microphone noise (§6).

**100% duty-cycle dropout.** If the 5 V input ever sags, the high-side FET turns on continuously and the output follows the input down. The minimum input to hold regulation is **[DOC §7.3.2]**:

```
VIN(MIN) = VOUT + IOUT × (RDS(ON) + RL)
```

At 1 A with `RDS(on)` = 100 mΩ and a 2.2 µH inductor DCR of, say, 100 mΩ **[INF — the inductor's DCR is not specified on the schematic]**:

```
VIN(MIN) = 3.315 + 1 × 0.2 = 3.5 V
```

So the 3.3 V rail survives a 5 V input dipping to ~3.5 V at 1 A. **This is a genuinely useful robustness margin** — a USB port sagging during a Wi-Fi TX burst will not brown out the board.

## 4. ⚠ The backlight is the load to worry about

The backlight circuit on sheet 1 is **[SCH]**:

```
3V3 ──── LEDA ──[ panel LED string ]── LEDK ──── R3 (3.9 Ω) ──── Q1 drain (AO3400A)
                                                                   Q1 source ── GND
                                    LCD_BLK (GPIO47) ── Q1 gate, with R4 10 kΩ gate-source pulldown
```

Note what is **not** there: **there is no inductor, no current-regulating LED driver, and no current-sense feedback.** The LED string runs directly from the 3.3 V rail through a single 3.9 Ω ballast resistor, switched by a MOSFET.

The consequence **[INF]**:

```
I_backlight = (3.315 - Vf_LED_string) / (3.9 + Rds_on(Q1) + R_dynamic_LED)
```

`Q1`'s `RDS(on)` is under 32 mΩ at `VGS` = 4.5 V and under 48 mΩ at 2.5 V **[DOC AO3400A]** — negligible. So the current is set almost entirely by the difference between 3.315 V and the LED forward voltage, divided by 3.9 Ω plus the LEDs' own dynamic resistance.

That difference is small. A white LED's `Vf` is typically 2.9–3.2 V, and it varies with binning, with drive current, and with temperature at roughly **−2 mV/°C**. Worked illustrations, treating the LEDs as an ideal `Vf` in series with 3.9 Ω:

| Assumed `Vf` | Current through 3.9 Ω |
|---:|---:|
| 3.20 V | 29 mA |
| 3.10 V | 55 mA |
| 3.00 V | 81 mA |
| 2.90 V | 106 mA |

**A 300 mV spread in `Vf` gives a 3.6× spread in current.** In reality the LEDs' own dynamic resistance flattens this considerably, so the real spread is smaller — but the point stands: **this is a ballast resistor, not a current source, and backlight current is not a well-controlled quantity on this board.** Two consequences:

1. **Brightness will vary board-to-board** with the LED bin, and **will drift upward as the panel warms** (`Vf` falls with temperature, so current rises — a mild positive-feedback effect, self-limited by the resistor).
2. **You cannot state the backlight current from the schematic.** It has to be measured. It is nonetheless the largest single steady load on the 3.3 V rail, so §5's budget treats it as a range.

The panel connector wires `LEDA` on **two** pins (6 and 8) and `LEDK` on **two** pins (2 and 4) **[SCH sheet 1]**, which normally indicates two parallel LED strings sharing one ballast resistor — a further reason not to assume a single-string current.

Backlight PWM is generated with LEDC: `LEDC_TIMER_3`, `LEDC_CHANNEL_1`, `LEDC_TIMER_8_BIT` (256 steps), **50 kHz**, `LEDC_SLOW_CLK_RC_FAST`, low-speed mode **[SRC, `lcd_bl_pwm_bsp.c`]**. See [alpha-and-omega-semiconductor/ao3400a](../../alpha-and-omega-semiconductor/ao3400a/README.md) for the switch side.

## 5. Power budget for the 3.3 V rail

The honest position: **no measured current figures for this board exist in any published Waveshare material.** What follows is a budget built from the datasheets of the parts on the rail, with every figure labelled. Use it to size a supply, not to predict a meter reading.

### 5.1 Estimated loads

| Load | Idle | Active / peak | Basis |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| ESP32-S3, CPU active, radio off | ~40 mA | ~50 mA | [DOC ESP32-S3 datasheet] |
| ESP32-S3, **Wi-Fi TX burst** | — | **~350 mA peak** | [DOC] — 802.11b at max output. Bursty, milliseconds |
| ESP32-U4WDH, CPU active | ~35 mA | ~50 mA | [DOC ESP32 datasheet] |
| ESP32-U4WDH, **Bluetooth Classic TX** | — | **~130 mA peak** | [DOC] |

### 5.2 Scenarios

| Scenario | Estimated 3V3 current | Headroom to 2 A |
|---|---:|---|
| Idle, backlight off, radios idle | **~90–100 mA** | 20× |
| Normal UI: backlight on, display redrawing, radios idle | **~180–250 mA** | 8× |
| **"Everything at once":** backlight 100% + full-screen redraw + Wi-Fi TX burst + BT Classic TX + SD write + haptic pulse | **~1.1–1.3 A** | ~1.6× |
| Absolute worst case, every peak coinciding | **~1.5 A** | 1.3× |

**Conclusion: the 2 A part is correctly sized, but the margin in the worst case is smaller than it first looks.** The design is not over-specified. Two practical recommendations follow:

- **Stagger your transients.** Firing a haptic pulse in the same millisecond as a Wi-Fi TX burst and an SD write is the worst case, and it is entirely avoidable in firmware. See [drv2605l §7](../../texas-instruments/drv2605l/README.md#7-initialisation-and-integration-gotchas).
- **The 3 A current limit is peak inductor current, not DC output.** With a 2.2 µH inductor at 1.5 MHz and 5 V→3.3 V, ripple current is roughly `(VIN−VOUT)×D/(L×fSW) = 1.685 × 0.663 / (2.2e-6 × 1.5e6) ≈ 339 mA` peak-to-peak, so peak inductor current sits about 170 mA above the DC output. At 1.5 A DC you are at ~1.67 A peak — still well under 3 A.

### 5.3 Thermal — the real constraint

`RθJA` for the SOT23-5 DBV package is **188.2 °C/W** **[DOC §6.4]**. That is a *high* thermal resistance; SOT23-5 has essentially no thermal path except its leads.

Dissipation, reading efficiency from the datasheet's Figure D008 (`VOUT` = 3.3 V curve peaks around 95% and stays above 90% from roughly 50 mA to 2 A) **[DOC]**:

| Output current | Efficiency (approx, from D008) | `Pout` | `Ploss` | **Junction rise above ambient** |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 100 mA | ~90% | 0.33 W | 0.037 W | **7 °C** |
| 250 mA | ~94% | 0.83 W | 0.053 W | **10 °C** |
| 500 mA | ~95% | 1.66 W | 0.087 W | **16 °C** |
| 1.0 A | ~94% | 3.32 W | 0.212 W | **40 °C** |
| 1.5 A | ~92% | 4.97 W | 0.432 W | **81 °C** |
| 2.0 A | ~90% | 6.63 W | 0.737 W | **139 °C** |

**[INF]** — arithmetic is ours; the efficiency figures are read off a datasheet graph and are approximate.

Read the bottom rows carefully. **At a sustained 2 A in a 25 °C room, the junction sits near 164 °C — above the 150 °C thermal shutdown.** The "2 A" headline rating is a silicon capability, not a thermal one in this package.

Two caveats that both point the same way:

- `RθJA` is a JEDEC test-board number (a multi-layer board with generous copper). This board is small and dense, so **the real figure is likely worse, not better.**
- It is inside a sealed handheld enclosure, so ambient at the package is not room temperature.

The practical envelope: **sustained loads up to ~500 mA are thermally uneventful; ~1 A is warm but fine; above ~1.2 A sustained, this package will get hot.** Since §5.2 puts sustained load at 180–250 mA and only the transients reach an amp, the design is comfortable — but do not, for example, add a 500 mA peripheral to this rail and assume the 2 A rating covers it.

## 6. Noise, and why the audio rail is separate

At 1.5 MHz the buck's fundamental is far above the 20 kHz audio band, and its harmonics are easy to filter. So why does the board bother with a separate LDO for the DAC?

Three reasons, in order of importance **[INF]**:

1. **PSRR falls off with frequency.** The PCM5100A's supply rejection is excellent at mains frequencies and mediocre in the hundreds-of-kHz range, where a buck's ripple and its load-transient response live.
2. **Load-transient-induced ripple is broadband.** Every Wi-Fi TX burst, SD write and haptic pulse is a step change on this rail. The regulator's response to a step is not at 1.5 MHz; it is a transient with energy spread down into the audio band. That lands directly in the analogue output.
3. **Power Save Mode makes the switching frequency variable and much lower at light load** (§3.1). Idle is exactly when you would be listening.

Hence: the **PCM5100A and CH445P run from `3V3_DAC`**, an [SGM2036-3.3](../../sgmicro/sgm2036-3.3/README.md) LDO fed from the 5 V rail through a `BLM18AG102SN1D` ferrite bead — a separate branch, not a post-regulator on the buck's output **[SCH sheet 5]**. That is a properly done split.

**But the microphone is on the buck rail, not the LDO rail** **[SCH sheet 4]**. If you are chasing a recording noise floor, that is where to look first. See [memsensing/msm261d4030h1cpm §6](../../memsensing/msm261d4030h1cpm/README.md#6-power-and-noise).

## 7. Measuring and monitoring

There is **no power-good pin** (§1) and **no current-sense shunt** on this rail **[SCH]**. What the board does give you is an ADC on the **5 V input**, not on 3.3 V:

- `BATT_ADC` → **ESP32-S3 GPIO1**, ADC1 channel 0, from a `R62`/`R63` **10 kΩ/10 kΩ** divider on the **5 V** net **[SCH sheet 4]**
- The vendor demo reads it with `adc_oneshot` + curve-fitting calibration at `ADC_ATTEN_DB_12`, 12-bit, and multiplies by 2 to undo the divider: `*value = 0.001 * vol * 2` **[SRC, `adc_bsp.c`]**

So `01_ADC_Test` reports the **5 V rail**, and its printed label is `"system voltage"` — accurately, if you read it carefully. Despite the net being called `BATT_ADC`, **it is not a direct battery-terminal measurement**; what it measures depends on the unidentified charger/power-path topology. See [generic/charger-power-path](../../generic/charger-power-path/README.md).

To measure the 3.3 V rail itself you need a probe on `C17` or on any 3V3 test point. To measure current you need to lift `L4` and insert a shunt.

## 8. Pitfalls

1. **Do not expect to switch this rail off.** `EN` is hard-tied. §2.1.
2. **Do not read "2 A" as a sustained thermal rating in SOT23-5.** §5.3.
3. **Do not treat `BATT_ADC` as a battery voltage.** §7.
4. **Do not assume a fixed backlight current.** §4.
5. **Do not add loads to `3V3` casually.** The header `CN1` exposes 5 V and GND but **not** 3V3 **[SCH sheet 1]** — which is a deliberate and sensible restriction. If you tap 3V3 from a test point instead, budget against §5.2, not against 2 A.
6. **`C17` is only 10 µF.** The datasheet's own advice for reducing the Power-Save-Mode output-voltage rise is to increase the output capacitance **[DOC §7.3.1]**. If you are adding a transient load, adding bulk capacitance near it is cheaper than anything else.
7. **The inductor's DCR is not specified in the schematic** — only "`L4` 2.2 µH". It matters for the dropout equation in §3.1 and for efficiency at high load. If you are rebuilding this circuit, choose a low-DCR 2.2 µH part rated well above 2 A saturation.

## 9. Alternatives

| Part | Why you might |
|---|---|
| **TLV62569DBV** (this) | 2 A, SOT23-5, 1.5 MHz, tiny external BOM. Good choice for the size |
| TLV62569**P**DDC | Same silicon plus a **power-good** output in SOT23-6. Would let firmware or a supervisor observe rail health. **The obvious upgrade if respinning** |
| TPS62825 / TPS62A01 | Higher current, better thermal packages |
| TPS63020 | Buck-**boost** — relevant if you ever want to run the 3.3 V rail directly from a single Li-ion cell rather than from a 5 V intermediate |
